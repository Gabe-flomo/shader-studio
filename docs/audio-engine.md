# Audio engine: Audio Unit synths and effects

The Play page's **Engine** tab is a set of **racks**. Each rack is an instrument, then effects, played from MIDI and the computer keyboard, with its own spectrum for audio readers:

```
MIDI / keyboard / keys on the track ─→ [instrument: AU synth, or the sample player] ─→ [AU effects…] ─→ rack mixer ─→ main mixer ─→ output device
a page sound (a send) ─────────────→ [input fed from the page]                    ─┘                       │             └─→ tap → WAV (a real-time recording)
                                                                                                          └─→ tap → FFT → `audio-engine://frame` → readers, the Listener's spectrum, the meters
```

In the **desktop app on a Mac** the racks run in a native engine (AVAudioEngine) that hosts **Audio Units** (v2 and v3), so any installed synth or effect works. In a **browser** a rack plays the **sample player** only, in Web Audio; Audio Unit slots are kept in the setup and play again on the desktop.

Everything here is **Pro**: `audio.engine` (racks, the sample player, engine readers) and `audio.plugins` (Audio Units, on racks and behind "+ Audio Unit effect"). On Free the record keeps its racks (nothing is dropped), the Engine tab shows the Pro card, and `playableForPlan` gives the engine no racks. **Upload sounds** in the Library stays Free: the sounds are drum pad samples too, which are Pro-layer features already, but keeping uploads open costs nothing and the Sounds tab was Free before.

The tab is **one view, laid out like Ableton Live's Arrangement** (docs/arrangement.md, "The Arrangement view"): a transport along the top, **a track per rack** (adding a rack is adding a track) with its lane on the bar/beat timeline, the **Master** track below them, and the selected track's **device chain** along the bottom. The separate Performance view (a card per rack) is gone; everything its cards had lives in the track header and the device chain. A setup makes up to **8 racks**, each **1 instrument + up to 8 effects** (a file made before these limits keeps what it has).

## The lead rack

With several racks and several MIDI inputs, not every rack plays at once. One rack **leads**: it takes the MIDI notes, the computer keyboard (the Computer keyboard toggle follows the lead) and pad hits (a pad controller's notes).

- **Select a track** (click its header or one of its clips; it's tinted in its colour) to make its rack the lead and show its device chain.
- **Lock as lead** (the track's ⋯ menu, or right-click its header) keeps that rack the lead while you select other tracks to edit them. The lead's header shows **Lead** (and **Locked**).
- Nothing selected: the **first rack** leads.
- A rack with **its own routing** (a MIDI device, or a channel, under Played by) plays what that sends, lead or not, as before. "No MIDI" plays nothing. A lead with its own routing plays only what that sends.
- The lock is kept in the record (`audioEngine.lock`); the selection is this session's (`useEngineSelection`). The rule is `rackPlays(rack, lead, device, channel)` and `leadRackId(ae, selected)` in `types/playAudioEngine.ts`; the host asks it for every MIDI note. The tape records what each rack receives, so the lead's track records and routed racks record theirs.

## The track and its device chain

**The track header** (left of its lane; above it on narrow panels and phones): a colour strip (the palette's by position, or picked in the ⋯ menu: `rack.color`), the instrument's icon, the name (double-click to rename), **Lead**, then **arm** (●), **M** (mutes the rack, live and on the tape), **S** (solo on the tape), a **volume** fader (0–2, double-click for 1) and a small **meter** (the rack's level). Drag a header to reorder the tracks (the ⋯ menu has Move up/down too); the ⋯ menu also has Rename, Colour, Lock as lead, Record this track alone, Clear its clips and Delete track.

**The device chain** (the bottom of the view, `DeviceChain.tsx`): the selected track's devices left to right, each a compact panel. Drag the handle between the lanes and the chain to give it more room; on a phone the chain scrolls sideways.

- **MIDI in** (or **Sound in**): **Played by**: any MIDI input (the default: a controller plugged in just plays, and so do the record's MIDI file and the MIDI node's keyboard stand-in), none, or one device by name; a channel or all (the default: an MPK mini's pads on channel 10 reach the rack along with its keys on channel 1). **Computer keyboard** gives the rack the computer keyboard (below). **Sound in** (desktop) sends a page sound instead (below). The strip of keys plays with the mouse (higher on a key hits harder; Shift is full velocity) and lights the notes typed.
- **The instrument**: **Choose an instrument…** (also offered straight away when a track is added) offers the **Sample player**, the **Granulator** (up to 64 grains from a sample, in Web Audio everywhere, desktop too; its own Sound effect chain, grain readouts as sensors, grains from a layer's particles: docs/granulator.md) and every enabled Audio Unit instrument (desktop). The sample player has **zones**: a Library sound on one key (drum-rack style from C2 = 36, the next free key each time) or across every key at its own pitch on C4. Each row sets the lowest key, the highest and the key where it plays unpitched. **Use the first 16 as a kit** fills C2… from the Library's sounds. A Granulator is followed by its **Sound effects** device (its Finish → Sound chain).
- **Effects**: **+ Add → Audio Unit effect…** appends one. Each has **on/off** (bypass), and moves by dragging it (or its ‹ › buttons); the order is the chain's order. An effect that isn't heard here (an Audio Unit in a browser, or after a Granulator) is dimmed and says why.
- On an Audio Unit device: **Open window** opens the plug-in's own view (AUv3 view controller, else the AUv2 Cocoa view, else Apple's generic view) in a native window (see Plug-in windows below); **Parameters** lists them. Every number, toggle and list is editable there, and the **+** makes it a Play control, target `au:<rackId>:<slotId>::<address>` (the instrument's slot id is `inst`). **Keep its settings** stores the unit's whole state (`fullState`, base64) in the setup, so a preset dialled in the plug-in's window comes back next time.
- **Configure** (the target icon) on the instrument or an effect picks up to 8 of its parameters as **rack controls**: faders on that device with their live values, Play controls in "<rack> · <slot>", mappable to MIDI knobs and recorded on the tape (docs/arrangement.md). On the desktop it works like Ableton's Configure: **touch a control in the plug-in to add it** (see Configure: touch to configure below); **Pick from list…** keeps the whole parameter list.
- **The Listener** (the audio readers, below).
- **+ Add**: an instrument (when there's none), an Audio Unit effect, a Listener, and for a Granulator its Sound effects.

**The Master track** (below the tracks): the engine's **mute**, **volume** (desktop; this device's settings, `shader-studio:audio:engine`) and a meter of every track together. Selecting it shows its devices: a **Listener** on the master, the engine's **Output** device (desktop), and the page's **Sound master chain** (Finish → Sound: each effect with its on/off and "Edit in Finish → Sound"). The browser's racks play through that chain; on the desktop, Audio Unit racks go straight to the engine's output, so the chain shapes only the page and the browser racks there (the master's header says so).

## Listeners: the audio readers as a device

A **Listener** is where the setup's audio readers listen (`audioReaders.input`, one place at a time): add it to a track (**+ Add → Listener**; readers listening elsewhere move here, their controls regrouped as **Audio readers · <track>**) or to the Master (`engine:master`, every rack summed: power per bin, `audioEngineHost.masterSpectrum`). It shows the spectrum; clicking it places readers exactly as on the Audio readers panel: each becomes a control in **Audio readers · <track>** with a reader → control mapping (docs/audio-readers.md), listed under the spectrum with live meters and a **Controls →** link; **Audio readers…** opens the panel. **×** removes it (the readers go back to the live input). In the panel, **Listen to** lists **Audio engine · <rack>** and **Audio engine · Master (every rack)**.

**Where it reads.** On a track the Listener sits among the effects (drag it, or ‹ ›): `audioEngine.listenAt`, the number of effects before it (absent: after the chain). The engines tap a rack at its end only (the desktop's FFT is on the rack's mixer, the browser's analyser after its volume), so a Listener reads exactly where it sits when nothing heard comes after it (`listenerExact`: the effects after it are bypassed, or not heard here: Audio Units in a browser, AU effects after a Granulator), and otherwise **after the whole chain, for now**; the device says which. A tap between effects needs a native tap per slot (not built yet). Exported websites read `engine:<rack>` Granulator racks only; `engine:master` reads nothing there.

## Computer keyboard

**Computer keyboard** in a track's MIDI in device plays the rack from the keyboard the way a DAW's musical typing does. Off by default, so a plugged-in controller is what plays; on for one rack at a time (turning it on for another rack turns it off here). While it's on and the Play page shows:

- **A W S E D F T G Y H U J K** are C, C#, D, D#, E, F, F#, G, G#, A, A#, B, C from the octave; **O L P ; '** carry on above. **Z / X** move the octave down and up, **C / V** the velocity by 16 (the MIDI in device has the octave and a velocity slider too), **Shift** sustains: keys let go keep sounding until Shift goes up.
- **The keyboard is the rack's**: every plain key is swallowed, so the app's shortcuts (F, A, U, T, the number filters…), the Play page's key mappings, drum pad keys, Space and the arrow keys don't fire. Combos with ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥ (Save, Undo, Rebuild…) still work, Tab still moves focus, and text fields still type normally.
- **A pill in the top bar** says "Keyboard → <rack> · Esc" while a rack has it. **Esc**, or clicking the pill, gives the keyboard back; so does removing the rack or leaving the Play page. All of these write `keyboard: false` into the rack, so the device's toggle follows.
- Notes go through the Play overlay like MIDI notes (`ae:<rackId>` pad actions), so a **take records them**.
- **In a plug-in window too** (desktop). With a plug-in's window focused, the same keys still play the rack: every key the plug-in's view doesn't use reaches its window (`AEPlugWindow`'s `keyDown:` at the end of the responder chain), which sends it to the page as `plugin-window:key` (`{ type: down|up|blur, code, key, repeat, shift, meta, ctrl, alt, window }`, the DOM event's fields) and swallows it; `lib/pluginWindowKeys.ts` hands it to the same `handleKeyDown`/`handleKeyUp` as a DOM key, so notes, octave, velocity, Shift sustain, Space (swallowed, as in the app) and **Esc** (gives the keyboard back) behave the same. Typing into a plug-in's text field isn't touched: the field takes the key first, and a Cocoa text view being edited is never forwarded; keys a plug-in handles itself stay its own. ⌘/⌃/⌥ combos, Tab and Return are never forwarded. A local event monitor sends the key-up of every key forwarded down (whoever gets it) and Shift; the window losing focus (or closing) sends `blur`, which lets go of held notes. Forwarding is on only while a rack has the keyboard (`ae_keys_forward`, `audio_engine/keys.rs`, from `rackKeyboard`'s `forward`). An out-of-process AUv3's view gets its keys in its own process; what it doesn't use may not come back, so there only the app's window plays.

Stored per rack as `rack.keyboard` (`types/playAudioEngine.ts`: `setRackKeyboard`, `keyboardRack`). The runtime is `lib/rackKeyboard.ts` (the key handling, the `useRackKeyboard` store the MIDI in device and pill read) and `lib/keyboardClaim.ts` (`keyboardClaimed(e)`, which every plain-key handler asks first). The host passes the record's keyboard rack to it in `frame()`; `audioEngineWire.ts` wires the record write-back.

The MIDI node's own **keyboard stand-in** (Studio, `midiEngine.setKeyboardEnabled`) is separate: it plays MIDI nodes and mappings, and reaches a rack on "any MIDI input" like a MIDI file would. It stands aside while a rack has the keyboard.

## Mapping and takes

A parameter control works like an audio effect's number: `parsePropTarget` gives the engine the prop id `au:<rack>:<slot>`, the mapping engine drives it (`playEngine.layerValue`), and every frame the host sends changed values to the native side, which glides numbers over 30 ms (`params.rs Smoother`) and jumps lists and toggles. A value moved on a device is kept in the record (`slot.params`) and put back when the unit loads, or when a mapping lets go. Removing a rack, an effect or an instrument removes the controls on it and their mappings (`withEngine`), and a loaded file keeps controls only for slots it has.

Notes go through the Play overlay as pad actions on `ae:<rackId>` (`pad` events, note + 1 as the amount, velocity 0..1, 0 for note off), so a **take records them** and plays them back into the rack; CC and pitch bend go straight to the rack and aren't recorded. Parameter controls are recorded like any control. A rack can **follow a Drum pad layer** (`rack.pads`): its hits (live, keys, MIDI, actions, a take) play note 36 + pad in the rack. **Finish → Sound → + Audio Unit effect** on a Drum pad layer's chain offers exactly that: it makes a rack with the layer's own samples, following the layer, and turns the layer's own Volume to 0 so the engine's version is what you hear.

## In renders and recordings (desktop)

The Record dialog lists each rack with an instrument or a send as a sound ("Audio engine · Rack 1") next to the songs; **Include** takes them all, and the same toggle leaves them out.

**Frame-by-frame renders (FFmpeg, the PNG sequence) of a take.** The racks are rendered offline, natively, in a second AVAudioEngine in manual rendering mode (nothing is heard), then put under the page's mix: `lib/engineRender.ts` turns the record and the take into a job — each rack as it is (units, presets, parameter values, sample player zones, volume, mute), every note the take recorded (`ae:<rack>` pad actions, and the hits of a drum pad layer the rack follows) as MIDI bytes at its second, and every parameter control on an `au:` target as steps every 10 ms where its value changed — and `ae_render_take` (`render.rs`) replays it **sample-exactly**: the render runs up to each event's sample, applies what is due there, and runs on (`schedule`). The reply is raw PCM (a u32 JSON header, then left and right as f32) and goes straight into the offline mix's destination, past the page's master chain, as live (`recordingAudio.ts`, `MixFx.engine`). A rack's units may report latency (`AUAudioUnit.latency`); the mix slides the render earlier by the largest (`engineRenderLead`). Without a take there is nothing to replay, and the dialog says so. A unit that won't load offline is left out with a note under the dialog ("… couldn't load offline; record it in real time instead"); the rest of the rack still renders. Renders run up to 90 s.

Measured with Apple's units (`cargo test --lib audio_engine::native -- --ignored`): a take with a note at 0.5 s and one at 1.25 s through DLSMusicDevice → AUDelay rendered at 48 kHz has RMS 0 before sample 23 800 and 0.037 in the 50 ms after sample 24 000; DLSMusicDevice and AUDelay report 0 latency, so no shift is applied. An input rack (a send) fed a sine renders it from frame 0 (no added latency offline).

**Real-time recordings.** When the recording starts, `ae_tap_start` puts a tap on the live engine's main mixer (after its Volume and mute) into a stereo ring; the worker thread drains the ring every 10 ms into a 16-bit WAV (`tap.rs`), zero-filling any stretch it missed so time never slips. When the recording is saved, `ae_tap_stop` finishes the file and `mux_recording_audio` runs FFmpeg to put it under the video (the picture copied, never re-encoded; mixed with the page's own sound track when there is one, else as the sound track), and the WAV is removed. **Alignment:** the page asks for the tap immediately before starting the recorder and notes both moments; the native side reports how long after the tap was installed its first buffer was rendered (`mach_absolute_time`, typically one I/O cycle: ≤ 11 ms at 512 frames); the WAV is shifted by the difference (`engineTapOffset`, `-itsoffset`). What is left is the recorder's own start-to-first-frame time, about a video frame; so the engine lands within roughly a frame of the picture and the page's sounds (which the recorder timestamps on its own clock). The engine's Output device latency is not compensated (neither is Web Audio's). A stretch the ring lost is silence, and the dialog says how much.

## Web sounds through Audio Units (sends, desktop)

**Sound in** (the MIDI in device, desktop) replaces the instrument with a page sound: **Everything the page plays** (the master bus, after the master chain) or one layer's sound (an audio layer's song, a Video layer's sound, a Drum pad layer: its chain, after the layer's own effects and before the master chain). Stored as `rack.source` ('master' or a chain id). What happens (`lib/engineSend.ts`):

```
the chain's output ─(audioFxHost.divert)─→ AudioWorklet (512 frames a chunk, resampled to the engine's rate) ─→ ae_rack_feed (raw bytes)
  ─→ the rack's input ring (AVAudioSourceNode) ─→ its AU effects ─→ rack mixer ─→ the engine's output
```

It is **one way**: the sound leaves the page's output (so it isn't heard twice) and its record bus (a real-time recording gets it from the engine's tap instead); the engine's output plays it. The rack's spectrum and readers hear it after the effects; the page's own readers on that sound keep reading it before the send. The ring starts with 21 ms of silence so a late chunk arrives late rather than as a hole; the Send device shows how much is buffered and counts dropouts. **Latency** one way, by construction: a 512-frame chunk (10.7 ms at 48 kHz) + the IPC hop (about 1–3 ms) + the 21 ms cushion + the engine's I/O buffer (about 11 ms) ≈ **45 ms**; this is computed, not measured on hardware (there is no hardware test here) — the Send device's buffer readout is the number to check. The web side needs an AudioWorklet (WKWebView has one). In a frame-by-frame render the sent tracks are mixed on their own first (a chain send with its chain's effects, without the master chain; a master send with everything) and uploaded as the rack's source (`ae_render_input`), so the render matches. Sends are desktop only; in a browser the rack is silent and the device says so. There is no way back into the page (the engine's sound stays in the engine); a true round trip would cost a second hop's latency and isn't offered.

## Plug-in windows

**Open window** on an Audio Unit device opens the plug-in's own view in a native window titled "Rack N · <plug-in>" (`ae_open_ui`; the plug-in window code in `src-tauri/native/audio_engine.m`).

- **Its own size.** The window's content is the size the plug-in asks for: an AUv3's `preferredContentSize`, else the loaded view's own size (an AUv2 Cocoa view's frame, the generic view's frame; a view laid out by constraints, its fitting size). Whole points, so it's crisp at any backing scale. The first time, it's centred on the screen of the app's window.
- **Resizing only as the plug-in allows.** The window resizes only along the axes the plug-in's view stretches: its autoresizing mask says it stretches *and* something in it grows when it does (the view is grown by 120×90, laid out, checked, and put back), and an AUv3 that answers its view configurations must accept a view half as big again. Apple's AUDelay and AUGraphicEQ resize both ways; the generic view (AUReverb2) and DLSMusicDevice only in width; AUNBandEQ not at all. A plug-in that doesn't stretch gets a fixed window (no resize handle), so there's no stretching it into empty space. A view bigger than the screen scrolls, and its window can grow up to the whole view.
- **The plug-in changing its size** (an AUv3's `preferredContentSize`, an AUv2 view's frame): the window follows, keeping its top-left corner, on screen. An out-of-process AUv3 often reports its size a moment after its view loads: the window opens at 480×320 and then snaps to it.
- **Remembered per plug-in** (by its component description, `type/subtype/manufacturer`) in the app's user defaults (`PlugInWindowFrames`): reopening puts the window back where it was, at the size it was when the plug-in resizes (its own size otherwise), always wholly on a screen and within its limits.
- **Following the host.** A value set from outside the window (a rack control, a mapping or its glide, the Parameters list, the tape) moves the plug-in's own knob. `ae_param_set` sets it through the unit's `AUParameterTree` (`setValue:originator:`, which reaches an AUv3 view's observers; while Configure watches, the watch's token is the originator so the watch isn't told), and for an AUv2 unit also posts `kAudioUnitEvent_ParameterValueChange` with `AUEventListenerNotify`, which is what classic Cocoa views listen to: the v2 bridge's own notification doesn't move them. Checked on the real views: without the post, AUDelay's Dry/Wet slider and Serum 2's OSC A Level knob stayed put; with it, both follow. Global-scope parameters only (addresses above 32 bits aren't v2 ids); Configure's watch still marks the set as the host's, so it's never a touch.
- **Close** closes the window only; the unit stays loaded and sounding. Opening an open plug-in's window brings it to the front (un-minimising it). Removing the slot or the rack closes its window.

## Configure: touch to configure

Turning Configure on for an Audio Unit (desktop) opens its window (or brings it to the front) and starts a **watch** on the slot (`ae_watch_start`); Done, or the device going (another track selected), stops it (`ae_watch_stop`). While it watches, any parameter the person moves in the plug-in's own window becomes a rack control at once: a row appears in Configure with its live value, and the row of the one being touched lights up. With 8 already, the first on the strip (the oldest unless reordered) makes room, its mappings and tape moves going with it, and a notice says "Replaced <name>" (Undo brings it back). The values reached while watching are kept in the setup when Configure closes. **Pick from list…** opens the full, filterable list: for plug-ins with no window, or ones whose window moves aren't reported.

How a touch is detected (`native/audio_engine.m` "Touch to configure", `touch.rs`):

- **Changes.** An observer on the unit's `AUParameterTree` (`tokenByAddingParameterObserver:`; an AUv2 is observed through the same bridge) queues every change the unit reports; each drain, about 33 a second on the worker thread, also compares every writable parameter with a snapshot, for units that don't tell observers. Read-only and output parameters (meters) are never watched. The host's own sets (the device, mappings, their glide, the tape) go with the watch's observer token as originator and are ignored for 0.3 s besides, so they're never mistaken for touches.
- **Is it the person?** A local event monitor notes clicks, drags and scrolls in the plug-in's own area of its window. A change is a touch while the button is held there (its release unseen: a knob's own tracking loop hides the drags, up to 30 s) or within a second of a click, drag or scroll. A parameter that moves twice with nobody touching (an LFO, an animated value) is "restless" and never added in this watch. When the window's events can't be seen at all (some out-of-process AUv3 views keep them), a change counts unless that parameter was already moving in the first second, before anyone could touch it. More than 3 new parameters moving at once is a preset change or a randomise: none are added.
- **To the page.** Each touched parameter is sent as `audio-engine:param-touched` `{ rack, slot, param, first }` (`param` as `ae_params` describes it, `value` the newest; at most one per parameter per drain). `lib/paramWatch.ts` parses it; `play/rackControls.ts` `touchRackControl` adds or replaces (one undo step); `RackControls.tsx` shows it.
- **Never during a render or a recording.** Render racks are never watched, touches are dropped while a take renders (`ae_render_take`), and the page ignores them while a take or the tape records or counts in (a knob turned then is being recorded, not configured).

If nothing arrives for 20 s, Configure says the plug-in may not report its window's moves and points to the list.

## Macros

Each rack has **8 Macro Controls**, like Ableton's rack macros: knobs that each turn any number of the rack's parameters (an Audio Unit's, the Granulator's settings; the sample player has none), each target through **its own range and curve**. The macro is what you map from outside, so a rack is automated through 8 knobs instead of many separate controls.

- **The Macros device** is first in the track's device chain. It starts **folded** (the collapsed-by-default rule): a summary ("2 in use · Wash 40% · …") and 8 thin value bars. Unfolded: 8 knobs (drag up and down, Shift for fine, arrow keys, double-click for 0), each with its value, its name (click to map it), **+** and **⋯**. A driven macro (a mapping, the tape) shows a dot riding its ring.
- **⋯** → **Map…**, **Learn (MIDI)** (the next knob, fader, key or bend becomes a mapping onto the macro), **Map a source…** (the **+** mini mapper: MIDI, an LFO, audio, a layer, another control, Increment…), **Rename…**, **Colour**, **On the Controls tab**.
- **Map** (below the knobs): the macro's targets, each with its **range** (the value at 0 and at 100%; typing past the parameter's own range is kept, per the slider rule; low above high **inverts** it, and ⇄ swaps the ends; **Full** puts back the parameter's whole range), its **curve** (Lin, Exp = x², Log = √x as on mappings, **S** = smoothstep, **Custom** breakpoints: click to add, drag, double-click to remove; joined by a monotone cubic, so it never overshoots) with a small preview and a dot where the macro is, **Detach as a control** and **Remove**. Add targets from the rack's **configured controls**, any device's **parameter list** (filterable), or on the desktop **Touch to add**: the plug-in's window opens and whatever you move there is added (the same watch Configure uses). Up to 16 targets a macro.
- **Rack controls and macros**: a Configure row's link button **Moves it to a macro**: it becomes a target over the control's range and leaves the strip (its Play control, mappings and tape moves go, as removing does; map the macro instead). **Detach as a control** turns a target back into a rack control at its current value (not when the device already has 8).
- **As a Play control**: a macro with a target (or once it's Learned or +'d) has a Play control on `macro:<rack>::<n>` labelled "Rack 1 · Macro 3" in the group "Rack 1 · Macros" (renames follow), 0..1. It's a mapping target and source like any control: MIDI, readers, Increment, quick link, the Controls tab, the Present page.

**How it runs** (`play/rackMacros.ts`, maths in `play/kit/macros.js` shared with the web runtime). Turning a macro by hand (its knob, its Controls tab fader) is one record edit: the macro's value and each target's value written into its slot's `params`, so the engine, a saved setup, a preset and a render all start there (and the Audio Units' knobs move at once, #387's path). While a mapping or the tape **drives** the macro, the mapping engine keeps its value under `macro:<rack>` / `<n>`; each frame the host (`audioEngineHost.frame`) reads parameters through `macroValueOf`, which gives a driven macro's target its curve-and-range value (else what drove it before: a rack control's own mapping, or the record), and sends what changed, the same batched glide path as rack controls. When the mapping lets go, the record's values come back.

**Takes, the tape, renders.** The tape records a macro's moves like a rack control's (`auto['macro:<rack>::<n>']`) and replays them as the macro's override; takes record the macro's control track. Renders expand it: the native job gets a parameter step for each Audio Unit target (`engineRender.ts` jobParams), a Granulator's settings read through the macro (`renderGrains`, lane previews). **Website exports**: browser instruments only (Granulator racks), their macros driven by the page's mappings (`SSKit.macros`).

**In the record**: `rack.macros` (`types/playAudioEngine.ts`), 8 of `{ id: 'm1'…, name, value 0..1, color?, targets: { slot, address, min, max, curve, points?, name?, lo?, hi? }[] }`. A rack saved before macros has none stored and reads as 8 empty ones (`rackMacros`); the first edit stores them. Targets on a slot that's removed go with it (`withEngine`); a file's targets on slots it lacks, or parameters a macro can't turn, are dropped. Saved with rack presets (docs/presets.md). Tests: `src/play/__tests__/rackMacros.test.ts`.

## Rack presets

A track's ⋯ menu → **Save as preset…** keeps the rack as a reusable piece: the instrument (an Audio Unit with its whole state, asked of the engine at that moment like **Keep its settings**; the sample player's zones; the Granulator's sample and settings), the effects in order with their state and on/off, the rack controls picked with Configure and their values, a Granulator's Sound effects, the Listener's place, MIDI input and channel, volume and colour. **No wiring**: no mappings onto its controls, no tape, no Grains from, no sends. **Add track → From a preset…** adds a rack from one; ⋯ → **Replace with preset…** puts one's devices on an existing track (its clips stay). An Audio Unit this Mac doesn't have is left out and named; the rest loads. A preset's Sound effects can also go on Finish → Sound's master chain. Saved under Files → Presets → Racks. See [presets.md](presets.md).

## Limits

- A plug-in that macOS can only load in the app's own process (an AUv2 its hosting service can't take, or with `AudioUnitsInProcess` on) can still take the app down when it crashes; see When a plug-in crashes below for what limits the damage.
- One output device for the whole engine; racks can't go to separate outputs.
- Sample player zones have no envelope or velocity curve, and up to 8 voices per rack.
- Renders replay only what a take recorded: CC and pitch bend aren't recorded, so a render doesn't have them; a note held across the take's start isn't heard (the render starts silent). Plug-ins that can't render offline are left out (noted); record in real time for those.
- The engine's Volume and mute are in a real-time recording (the tap is after them); a muted engine records silence. A frame-by-frame render ignores the engine's master Volume (rack volumes apply).
- A send's latency (≈ 45 ms) is not compensated anywhere: a drum pad layer sent through a rack sounds that much after its pad lights.
- Touch to configure relies on the plug-in reporting its window's moves to the host (observer or value change); one that keeps its UI state private can't be touched in, only picked from the list. A plug-in that sets several parameters from one knob (a macro) adds each of them (up to 3 at once).

## When a plug-in crashes

Third-party plug-ins crash sometimes (a bad update, a licence check, a sample library it can't find). The engine keeps that from costing your work and from happening twice (`src-tauri/src/audio_engine/safety.rs`, the loading code in `native/audio_engine.m`):

1. **Out of process where macOS can.** Every unit is instantiated with `kAudioComponentInstantiation_LoadOutOfProcess`. An AUv3 always runs in its extension's process; an AUv2 does too on macOS 11 and later when the system's Audio Unit hosting service can take it. A crash there ends that process, not Playfield: the rack goes silent and the card reports the error. If an out-of-process load fails outright it's tried once in-process (some AUv2s refuse to leave). The load is asynchronous with a 10 s limit: a unit that doesn't answer is a failed load, never a hung app. `ae_status` says whether the last unit ended up in-process (`lastInProcess`). To go back to in-process loading for everything: `defaults write com.shaderstudio.app AudioUnitsInProcess -bool YES` (or `PLAYFIELD_AU_IN_PROCESS=1`).
2. **Tried out before it's used.** The first time a third-party unit is loaded, and again whenever it's updated (its version or its bundle's date changes), it's first loaded in a **throwaway copy of the app** (`<app binary> --au-probe <type> <subtype> <manufacturer>`: no window, no Dock icon) that does what the engine does: instantiate it the same way, list its parameters, read and restore its preset, and render a note through it offline (nothing is heard). A toast says "Trying Kontakt 7 out first…". If that process crashes, or doesn't finish in **45 s**, the unit is marked (crashed, or didn't finish) and the app **refuses to load it**: a toast "Kontakt 7 wasn't loaded" with **Try again**, and it's switched off in Settings → Plugins. An error that isn't a crash (it says it can't load) doesn't block it. Results are kept per unit and version in `plugin-probes.json` in the app's data folder; Apple's own units are trusted and never tried out.
3. **The loading marker.** Right before a unit is instantiated, its window is opened or its preset is restored, `loading-plugin.json` (`{ name, code, at, stage, version }`) is written and synced; right after, it's removed (a window's stays 5 s, while its view loads). Finding it at launch means that plug-in took the app down: it's recorded as crashed for that version, switched off in the Plugins setting with a note ("Crashed Playfield while loading"), and named in crash recovery's dialog: "Playfield closed unexpectedly while loading Kontakt 7, with “Untitled” open (last autosaved 12:41)" (docs/crash-recovery.md).
4. **What can be caught is caught.** Loading, view creation, preset save and restore and the parameter list are wrapped so an Objective-C exception becomes an error on the card, not a crash. After every render of every unit its output is checked: a buffer with a NaN or an infinity is **flushed to silence** before the next unit or the mixer hears it (a filter fed a NaN never recovers), and counted (`ae_status` `nanFlushes`).
5. **Try again.** Files → App settings → Autosave and recovery lists the plug-ins switched off for crashing, each with **Try again** (runs the trial now; if it passes, the plug-in is switched on again) and, for one that didn't finish in time, **Load anyway** (it may be waiting for a licence dialog the trial can't show; it's then loaded without a trial). Updating the plug-in also gives it a fresh trial automatically.

**What's still possible:** a plug-in that only loads in-process and crashes *after* its trial passed (while playing, or when you touch its window) still takes the app down. The loading marker attributes a crash during load, window opening or preset restore; any crash is covered by autosave and recovery, so the work comes back either way. A render of a take skips a unit known to crash (noted in the render's notes) but doesn't write the marker.

## Plugins (Library → Settings → Plugins)

Every installed Audio Unit (instruments and effects, with maker, kind, version, AUv2/AUv3) with a switch: only the ones switched on appear in the instrument and effect pickers (a rack already using a hidden one keeps it). Search, **Enable all** / **Disable all** (of what the search shows), **Rescan plugins**, a count. A rescan tags newly found units **New** until the list is closed; **New plugins start disabled** makes them start off. Kept per device as `shader-studio:audio:plugins` (Files → App settings → Camera, MIDI, OSC and audio → Audio Unit plugins; resetting offers every unit again). `lib/pluginSettings.ts`.

## For the owner to test on hardware

1. Build the desktop app; on first launch open Library → Settings → **Plugins** and check the list has Apple's units (DLSMusicDevice, AUSampler, AUDelay, AUReverb2, AULowpass…) and your third-party ones. Hide one and check it leaves the pickers.
2. Engine tab → **Add track** → **DLSMusicDevice**; play the keys in its MIDI in device and a MIDI keyboard: sound from the chosen output, the spectrum moving. + Add → Listener → place a reader → map it to a control.
   Then **Computer keyboard** in MIDI in: the top bar shows "Keyboard → Rack 1 · Esc", A–K play, F no longer fits the view, ⌘Z still undoes; Esc gives it back and the toggle goes off.
3. Add **AUDelay** and **AUReverb2**; open their windows, reorder, bypass. **Plug-in windows**: each opens at its own size with no empty space around it; AUDelay resizes (its graph grows), AUReverb2 only gets wider, AUNBandEQ can't be resized. Move and resize one, close it, reopen: same place and size. Open it twice: one window, brought to the front. On a Retina screen, check the controls are sharp. Move a parameter, make it a control, map an LFO to it: it should glide, not click.
4. A third-party AUv2 (a signed build needs the entitlement; an ad-hoc dev build loads them anyway) and an AUv3 (it appears with "AUv3"; its own view should open in the window). The window should hug the plug-in's UI: no dark border, no stretching a fixed-size plug-in; one with a resize corner (or a size menu) should resize its window, and one whose editor is resizable should let you drag the window's edge. If one opens at 480×320 and doesn't snap to its size, or stretches into empty space, note the plug-in's name and format.
5. Keep its settings → save the setup → reopen: the plug-in comes back as dialled.
6. Upload sounds in the Library → a sample player rack → "Use the first 16 as a kit" → play from a pad controller.
7. Record a take while playing a rack; play it back.
8. Change the output device while playing; unplug it.
9. **Touch to configure.** On a rack with an instrument, click Configure (the target icon): the plug-in's window opens (or comes to the front) and Configure says "Touch a control in the plug-in to add it". Turn a knob in the window: it appears at once as "1. <name>" with its value following; turn a second: "2. …". Turn the first again: no duplicate, its row lights. Touch 9 different ones: the ninth replaces the first with a "Replaced <name>" notice; ⌘Z brings it back. Change the plug-in's preset from its own menu: nothing is added. Try Apple's AUDelay and AULowpass (generic view), a third-party AUv2 with its own UI, and an AUv3; for each, note whether touches arrive (if one never adds anything, Configure says so after 20 s; note the plug-in and format). A synth with an LFO or an animated display: its moving parameters shouldn't be added on their own. Start a take or tape recording with Configure open and turn a knob: nothing is added. Done: the faders on the device show where you left them. **Pick from list…** still opens the full list.
   **A rack control moves the plug-in's knob.** With Configure's controls on **AUDelay** (Dry/Wet) and on **Serum** (e.g. OSC A Level, Main Vol), open the plug-in's window and drag the rack control's fader, or turn a mapped MIDI knob: the plug-in's own slider/knob follows as you move it. Do it with Configure still on (Done not pressed): nothing new is added as a touch.
   **Keys in a plug-in window.** Turn on Computer keyboard for the rack, open Serum's (or AUDelay's) window and click on it so it's focused: A–K play, Z/X and C/V change octave and velocity (the MIDI in device shows it), Shift sustains, Esc gives the keyboard back (the pill goes). Click a text field in the plug-in (Serum's preset name / browser search) and type: letters go into the field, no notes. Hold A, click back on the app's window: the note stops. ⌘W / ⌘Q still work in the plug-in window.
9. **Render the take** (Record → the take → Encode with FFmpeg, Include sounds on): the video's sound has the rack's notes where you played them, with the delay and reverb; scrub to a hit and check it lands on the frame. Make a control of a parameter, wiggle it while recording another take, render: the sweep is in the sound. With a song playing too: both in the mix, the song through its chain, the rack straight in.
10. **Record in real time** with a rack sounding (and a song): the file has the rack's sound; the dialog says "Adding the Audio engine's sound…" at the end. Play the video against the live sound in your head: the racks and the song should line up (within a frame). Try with the engine muted (silence), and a longer recording (a minute) for dropouts.
11. **Sound in**: on a rack with AUDelay and AUReverb2, choose a Drum pad layer (or Everything the page plays). The pads now sound through the engine's output only, with the effects; the card shows about 20–40 ms buffered and no dropouts. Listen for the latency (a pad hit lands about 45 ms late), clicks (dropouts count up), and that switching back to "Its instrument" restores the page's sound. Render a take of it: the same sound offline.
12. **When a plug-in crashes.** (a) Add **Serum** to a rack: the first time, a toast "Trying Serum out first…", then it loads as before (a second or two later than usual). Quit and relaunch, add it again: no trial this time. (b) Add **Kontakt 7**: after its trial, either it loads (it didn't crash in the trial: check Activity Monitor for "AUHostingService" or the Kontakt extension process, which means it runs out of process and can't take Playfield down), or it's refused with "Kontakt 7 wasn't loaded… Try again". If the trial times out (a licence or Native Access dialog it can't show), use **Load anyway** in Files → App settings → Autosave and recovery. (c) If Playfield does go down while loading or opening a plug-in: relaunch; the Recover dialog should name the plug-in, recovering should bring the project back, and the plug-in should be switched off in Plugins and listed with Try again in App settings. (d) Switch a plug-in off in Settings → Plugins: it leaves the pickers as before, and a rack already using it keeps it (the switch hides; it doesn't refuse). (e) Tell me the plug-in names and whether each ended up out of process (`lastInProcess` in `ae_status`, or the processes in Activity Monitor).
